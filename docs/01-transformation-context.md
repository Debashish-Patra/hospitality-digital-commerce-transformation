## Executive framing

A complex hospitality business serves guests across a lifecycle that begins with inspiration and research, continues through booking and the stay, and extends into loyalty and future travel. The underlying business, however, may operate through separate processes, technology platforms, product definitions and property-level procedures.

The strategic opportunity is to connect those parts into a coherent digital commerce experience—while ensuring that the business can fulfil what it sells.

> **Transformation question:** How can an operating-model and reservation-platform transformation create the foundations for better customer experiences and more effective product commercialization?

## The problem to solve

The following are common problem hypotheses for a hospitality commerce transformation. They are not assertions that every issue existed in the original engagement.

| Dimension | Potential friction | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| Business processes | Different teams and properties may follow different workflows | Define common principles while preserving necessary local flexibility |
| Reservation technology | Legacy capabilities and integrations may constrain change | Establish a scalable target-state capability model |
| Product information | Product descriptions, terms, pricing and availability may be fragmented | Create consistent product definitions and governance |
| Digital journey | Room and ancillary products may not be equally discoverable or purchasable | Design around customer intent rather than internal systems |
| Fulfillment | A digital purchase may depend on property operations and inventory | Validate operational readiness before activating offers |
| Loyalty | Benefits and eligibility can be difficult to explain consistently | Make value, eligibility and fulfillment transparent |
| Measurement | Technology delivery and commercial outcomes may be tracked separately | Connect releases to adoption, customer outcomes and business performance |

## Desired future state

The target is not one screen or one system. It is an integrated operating and commerce model in which:

1. Business processes and accountabilities are clear.
2. Reservation capabilities support the intended product and transaction journeys.
3. Product information, pricing and availability are governed consistently.
4. Customers can discover and purchase eligible products with transparent terms.
5. Property teams can fulfil the promise.
6. Loyalty and communications reinforce the experience.
7. Performance data informs continuous improvement.

## Strategic outcomes

### Customer value
- Reduce friction in search, comparison, booking and pre-arrival planning.
- Make relevant room options, add-ons and loyalty benefits easier to understand.
- Set clear expectations about eligibility, availability, terms and fulfillment.

### Commercial value
- Improve discovery and conversion of eligible products.
- Create structured opportunities for room upgrades and ancillary-product sales.
- Reduce avoidable abandonment caused by uncertainty or fragmented journeys.

### Operational value
- Reduce manual work and ambiguity across process handoffs.
- Improve the consistency of product, pricing and fulfillment rules.
- Make ownership and exception handling explicit.

## Scope boundaries

A transformation of this kind has several connected but distinct layers:

- **Operating model:** roles, processes, governance, exception handling and readiness.
- **Platform:** reservation, product data, pricing, inventory, payments and integration capabilities.
- **Experience:** search, product detail, checkout, pre-arrival and in-stay interactions.
- **Commercialization:** assortment, proposition, merchandising, channels, communication and launch.
- **Measurement:** customer, commercial, operational and technical outcomes.

## Strategic principle

**Start with the business and customer outcomes; then define the operating model and platform capabilities required to deliver them.**
